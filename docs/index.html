Screening cut-offs

A cell is optimal if it passes every optimal cut-off, sub-optimal if it passes every sub-optimal one, otherwise non-viable.

How this works, and what to trust

The published cut-offs

Zones are recomputed, not looked up

Every time you move a control the app reclassifies the whole aquifer from the underlying property grids — porosity, CO₂ density, CO₂ phase, fault setback class and overpressure flag — exactly as make_optimal_zone_map.py does. Nothing is precomputed, so any combination of cut-offs is fair game, including ones the paper never tried.

The grids are packed into one RGB image per aquifer: red carries porosity in 0.2 % steps, green carries CO₂ density in 4 kg/m³ steps, and blue carries the fault class, phase, overpressure and footprint flags as bits. They are resampled to a 400 m grid from the archived 200 m grids, which keeps each aquifer around 150–300 KB and the reclassification under about 15 ms.

How faithfully does it reproduce the paper?

Two things the archived data says that the summary table does not

Capacity

M_CO2 = A × h × NTG × φ × (1 − S_wirr) × E × ρ_CO2

Area, porosity and CO₂ density come from your live zone; thickness and NTG are the published per-aquifer values from well petrophysics. Swirr = 0.27 and E = 0.02 follow the paper, which gives their means but not their standard deviations — this tool uses ± 0.05 and ± 0.01 respectively, the latter chosen because it reproduces the published P10–P50–P90 most closely. Percentiles use the paper's exceedance convention, so P10 is the high estimate.

What this does not do

  • No connected-area clustering. The paper discards optimal zones smaller than 100 km² via DBSCAN; areas here are the raw classified total, so they run slightly higher.
  • No caprock integrity, injectivity or permeability assessment — out of scope in the paper too.
  • Porosity is the basin-wide depth trend, not a facies model, so it cannot capture local reservoir quality.
